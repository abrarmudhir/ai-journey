# 5. Presentation transcript

## LinkedIn Pathways

**Audience:** Executive stakeholder  
**Target duration:** 9:00 to 9:45 at a measured pace  
**Scenario:** LinkedIn launches a personalised career-growth platform for professionals who need a clearer route from current skills to their next role.

> Recording note: the Udacity submission still requires the student to record and submit their own video with voiceover. This transcript supports that recording but does not replace it.

## Slide 1 — LinkedIn Pathways

“Good morning. I’m proposing LinkedIn Pathways, a personalised career-growth platform that helps professionals see the next credible step in their career, then make measurable progress toward it. The idea builds on LinkedIn’s existing professional graph and turns it into a practical development plan.”

## Slide 2 — Executive Summary

“Career growth is surrounded by content, but people still struggle to decide what to do next. Pathways connects a member’s profile, career goal, skills gap, learning options and professional network in one plan. I’m asking for approval to test a 12-month MVP with one million invited members and fifty employer partners.”

## Slide 3 — Business Case

“I’ll start with the problem, then show the market, the proposal, the economics and the roadmap. The key decision is whether LinkedIn should invest in helping members navigate career change, not simply consume more content.”

## Slide 4 — Initial Focus

“LinkedIn already has signals about a member’s skills, interests and professional graph. Yet career growth often still feels like guesswork. The emotional cost is simple: people can spend months learning without knowing whether they are moving closer to the role they want. The World Economic Forum estimates that 39 percent of workers’ current skill sets may change or become outdated between 2025 and 2030. We should make the next step clearer. My ask is approval for discovery and a focused MVP.”

## Slide 5 — Opportunity

“The opportunity is large, but the first wedge is intentionally small. LinkedIn reported 1.2 billion members in its 2025 business highlights. Coursera reported 197 million registered learners at the end of 2025, which confirms demand for career-linked learning. We will not target everyone. We will start with ten million English-speaking mid-career professionals across the UK, US and Canada, invite one million in year one and aim to activate one hundred thousand. The sixty-dollar annual net revenue number is a model assumption, not an observed fact.”

## Slide 6 — Proposal

“Pathways has four jobs. First, diagnose the gap between a member’s current profile and the target role. Second, recommend a ranked set of courses, projects, credentials and people to meet. Third, coach the member through weekly actions. Fourth, turn completed work into credible evidence on the profile. The target user is a mid-career professional changing role or industry. The product is specific because it joins planning, action and proof.”

## Slide 7 — Return on Investment

“The business case is deliberately simple. I’m assuming six million dollars for a twelve-month team covering product, data science, design, partnerships and go-to-market. At one hundred thousand activated members and sixty dollars of annual net revenue per member, the base case produces six million dollars, or zero percent first-year ROI. At one hundred and fifty thousand activated members, revenue reaches nine million dollars and ROI reaches fifty percent. We scale only if activation exceeds ten percent and ninety-day return exceeds thirty-five percent.”

## Slide 8 — Measurement

“The first-year scorecard measures both business value and user value. We want one million invited members and one hundred thousand activated. We want thirty-five percent of activated members to return within ninety days, twenty-five percent to complete a recommended action within thirty days and fifteen percent to report a meaningful career outcome within six months. We will also recruit fifty employer partners. Recommendation quality, fairness, privacy and partner conversion are guardrails.”

## Slide 9 — Competitors

“The market is validated. The question is where LinkedIn can create a defensible position. Coursera proves that people will use a large learning marketplace. LinkedIn Learning and Udemy prove that course discovery, credentials and enterprise plans are familiar behaviours. Pathways must win by connecting those behaviours to a member’s goal, live labour-market signals and evidence of progress.”

## Slide 10 — Coursera

“Coursera’s strength is breadth and credibility: university and industry courses, professional certificates, degrees, skills tracks and consumer and enterprise plans. Its reported 197 million registered learners show meaningful scale. The gap for LinkedIn is contextual. Coursera helps a learner find learning. LinkedIn can help a professional decide which learning matters for a target role and which opportunity should come next.”

## Slide 11 — LinkedIn Learning and Udemy

“Adjacent products already offer video courses, assessments, recommendations, certificates, badges and enterprise learning plans. Those features are useful, but they are still fragmented from the member’s career narrative. Pathways is the layer that links the plan to the target role, the network and evidence of progress. That is the difference between a catalogue and a career operating layer.”

## Slide 12 — Our Advantages

“Why LinkedIn? First, the professional graph is first-party and already connects people, skills, roles and companies. Second, LinkedIn can use live labour-market signals rather than a static curriculum. Third, completed work can become portable evidence on the member profile. Finally, the same system can create value for employers by surfacing emerging skills. These advantages are harder to copy than price or a larger course catalogue.”

## Slide 13 — Roadmap and Vision

“The vision is that every professional can see the next credible step in their career and make measurable progress toward it. The first year focuses on two themes: personalised skill intelligence, and trusted pathways with momentum. Employer signal and internal mobility come after the member loop is proven.”

## Slide 14 — Roadmap Pillars

“The roadmap follows the vision. We first make the gap understandable, then make progress practical, then extend the value to employers. This sequencing matters. It protects the MVP from becoming a broad marketplace before we know whether members will act on a personalised plan.”

## Slide 15 — Theme 1: Personalised Skill Intelligence

“The first theme is skill intelligence. The goal-to-skill map translates a target role into a shortfall view. The market pulse refreshes priority skills from current role and hiring data. The next-best-action feature ranks one practical learning or networking step each week. Together, these features make recommendations explainable and useful.”

## Slide 16 — Theme 2: Trusted Pathways and Momentum

“The second theme turns advice into sustained behaviour. Curated pathways combine LinkedIn Learning, external partners and member-created projects. Proof of work captures assessed outputs, endorsements and verified credentials. The progress coach uses reminders, reflection and outcomes to adapt the plan. The product earns trust by showing both what to do and why it matters.”

## Slide 17 — Theme 3: Employer Signal and Internal Mobility

“The third theme follows after member product-market fit. Skills-first matching helps employers search beyond titles and degrees. An internal opportunity exchange surfaces adjacent roles and projects. Partner analytics show which pathways lead to interviews, moves and hires. This creates a second side of the value proposition without making employers the first dependency.”

## Slide 18 — Widening the Scope

“If the member loop works, Pathways can expand into employer-sponsored development plans, internal mobility, credential verification, and career transitions for students and returners. Those are attractive possibilities, but they are not the first release. The immediate decision is narrower: fund discovery, recruit design partners and return with a tested MVP gate in ninety days.”

## Slide 19 — Close

“To close, the problem is urgent, the distribution advantage is real and the first bet is measurable. Pathways gives members a clearer route from skills to work while strengthening LinkedIn’s role in the professional journey. I’m asking for approval to begin discovery and validate the activation, retention and outcome assumptions before we scale.”
