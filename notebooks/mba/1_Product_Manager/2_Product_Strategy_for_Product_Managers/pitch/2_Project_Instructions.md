# Project Instructions

In this project, your goal will be to research this opportunity, create a pitch deck for the product you hope to build, and then present it.

Let's walk through the project requirements. Make a copy of the [Pitch a Product Vision Project Starter Template](Pitch%20a%20Product%20Vision%20Project%20Starter%20Template.pptx) (opens in a new tab) or download the PPT template from the **Downloads** section of this project. Complete each section to create a pitch deck for the product you hope to build.

## Initial Focus

**Start by identifying problems to solve by creating a compelling story using evidence and metrics.**

Highlight why you chose this focus. It can be as simple as "This was assigned to me by my boss" or "This matches the company goals." This isn't just a fun product to build. It should be a product that solves someone's pain.

For example, in our sample project, we articulate the pain of "we are wasting 33 million a year in health care costs on diabetes care that could be saved". That's pain, and someone might be interested in solving it.

## Opportunity

**Next, calculate the total addressable market (TAM) for your product.**

Give details on how big or small this problem is from a market perspective. You need to quantify the size of the opportunity. It can't be as all-encompassing as "the market for diabetes treatment is $300B".

You need to provide at least two stats from your research to support your claim. The Total Addressable Market (TAM) is one; others could be competitor revenue, market saturation, market population, or others.

TAM should usually be counted in billions. The market opportunity needs to be smaller and more realistic. TAM should be measured in billions, but the initial focus area should be measured in tens of millions at most.

You should use links and sources where possible. This doesn't need to be comprehensively footnoted, but quotes and numbers are always more believable when the source is mentioned.

## Proposal

**Define product use cases and target user(s):**

State what you want to build. Give brief details at a high level on functionality, just enough for readers to understand the proposal.

It can't be vague, like "I will build a marketplace!" It has to be specific. What kind of marketplace? What does it do? How is it special? What audience does it address?

## ROI

**Calculate the return on investment:**

- What do you think this will cost?
- What will this produce in terms of revenue or cost savings?
- What is the return on investment?

You don't need to provide an extremely detailed business case here. That's usually provided as supporting material. Detailed spreadsheets are often too brittle because they rely on numerous assumptions, any one of which being wrong will throw the entire ROI calculation off.

Think of simple calculations instead. X months of development * 10k each / some simple measure of return.

## Measurement

**Define success metrics:**

Identify the specific success metrics for your product during the first year and how they will be measured. Please provide specific KPIs.

For example, the percentage of cost reduction after a specific timeframe or reduction in support tickets or engagement percentage on the app. Elaborate further on similar metrics.

## Competitors

**Compare the features of competitor products/solutions:**

You need to outline at least one competitor. For each competitor, give a brief overview of at least four features. Provide enough so that the audience can understand their offering.

What does this competitor do? What features do they have? Do they see good market penetration? Any stats you can give on revenue?

Remember that these don't need to be exact competitors but might be companies in the same field. For example, YouTube competitors might be Amazon Video, but they may also be traditional TV networks. You may want to highlight a category of competitors instead of a specific one (e.g., Traditional TV networks as opposed to NBC).

## Advantages

**Define product positioning and opportunity in the market:**

Then follow this up with a slide outlining why your product is better. This slide should answer the question, "why us?" Why should a buyer choose your product instead of one of the competitors?

Remember that the best competitive advantages are hard for competitors to match. For example, network effects, unique business partnerships, and unique content are complex for competitors to match. Pricing is not.

## Roadmap Pillars

### Define and craft a compelling vision and strategy

What is your vision? What's the big picture, a bold statement of where you want this to go?

Is there a strategy behind the roadmap or simply a list of follow-up items? A strategy means a goal. For example, your strategy might be to expand into other geographical markets by investing in UI translations and regional-specific features.

You need to provide at least two themes corresponding to the future strategy of the product. No more than three themes. Anything else, and your roadmap becomes diluted.

### Build a roadmap around the identified strategic areas

For each strategic theme, provide two or three features (no more than three) that you want to develop for this theme, along with a brief explanation of each.

The roadmap should be detailed. It's easy to attach themes to a roadmap, such as "It's better reporting," but the list of features on the roadmap should flow from your vision and strategies.

## Create a Compelling Presentation and Be Persuasive

- **Use the provided template and fill out the slides applicable to your product.** Don't try to invent a new process where one already exists. Don't try to make what you're doing "special." Follow the process. In this case, we are providing you with a template. In real-life scenarios, you can find a template through other PMs at your company. Don't reinvent the wheel, and don't bring an unfamiliar template to your stakeholders if they're used to information being presented a certain way. It's distracting and will detract from your message.
- **Pay attention to the way you craft your presentation.** This also means how your slides are created visually.

### General rules for slides

- Each slide should have a key takeaway.
- Content on each slide should be focused on supporting the key takeaway.
- A slide shouldn't have more than five top-level bullets. Each top-level bullet should not have more than three sub-bullets.
- Each bullet or sub-bullet should not have more than one complete sentence. Preferably, each bullet or sub-bullet is just a sentence fragment.
- Formatting (font, sizes, etc.) should be consistent. Be careful of the text you copied and pasted into the presentation. It will frequently carry over its formatting.
- Use at least three visuals (like graphs and images) in appropriate places to help readers understand your message.

Use the three different types of persuasion when building and delivering your presentation:

- **Credibility:** Use and cite credible sources when appropriate (at least 2) and present at least two numbers obtained from outside sources. The sources should be current and reputable.
- **Emotion:** Include a message that makes your audience feel a specific way. This could be humor or highlighting pain points that users are experiencing.
- **Logic:** Present facts. You should present fact-based data.

## Present a 10-Minute Overview of the Product Opportunity to an Executive Stakeholder

- Create a video recording of yourself giving your presentation. You'll want to make sure to do a screen capture of your slides and the audio of you explaining each slide.

### Creating a Video Recording

- On a Mac, you can use QuickTime Player.
- You can use the built-in Screen Recording Tool (Windows 10) on a PC. There are also many other free apps available for recording your screen and audio (including Zoom).
- **Note:** Make sure you are also capturing audio from your microphone.

- Remember, your presentation should be 10 minutes and no longer. Pitch presentations are usually short on time, and you must practice succinctly delivering your idea.
- Transitions - Presenters should smoothly transition between bullet points and between slides. The presentation should have a nice flowing narrative that moves from topic to topic and connects the topics.
- Reading - Do you find yourself reading the slide verbatim? This can be improved. The slides should be a summary, not a script. You need to know your product. If it feels like you're reading bullet points, people will lose confidence. And watch out for pauses (unless for dramatic effect).
- Do you sound positive and confident when talking about your product idea? You are the cheerleader for your product. You are always optimistic about it. You always believe in it. If you don't, no one will.
- Did you structure your narrative and thoughts, or did their delivery seem disjointed? For example, a slide should start with a core statement, then add a few summary bullet points that support the core statement, and finish with a strong statement that can help transition to the next slide.

Yes, this is subjective, but it isn't also necessary. Think about a good public speaker you've seen, perhaps a TED talk you reviewed; how engaging were you? Did you find yourself easily distracted from their presentation, or were you captivated by their narrative thread?

The real question here is, "Are you convincing?" Did you sound like you believed in the product you were pitching? Do you seem to believe this is a good product? You're looking for the same signs you were in the previous row.
