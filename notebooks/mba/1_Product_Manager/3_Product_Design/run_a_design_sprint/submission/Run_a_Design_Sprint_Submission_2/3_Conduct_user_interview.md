## Ready-to-read interview script — approximately 30 minutes

This is a moderator script for conducting the session, not a transcript of an interview that has already happened. Read the moderator lines aloud. Let a real participant respond in their own words and use the response spaces for notes. After recording, transcribe the actual conversation to create the exact interview transcript. Do not supply answers for the participant or ask someone to perform the fictional findings.

### Before the session

- Recruit a mid-career LinkedIn member considering a promotion or career transition, preferably with a target role and recent online-learning experience.
- Open the Career Compass prototype linked in the presentation and check that the participant can use it.
- Have the recording application and this document ready. Capture both voices clearly. Keep a backup of the original audio.
- Use participant code P01 in the submission rather than their full name.
- Before starting the recording, explain who will receive it and agree on the sharing method. A link accessible to anyone who has it must be explained before obtaining permission. If that is unsuitable, agree to restricted reviewer access instead.

### 1. Welcome and recording permission — 3 minutes

**Moderator, before recording:**

“Thank you for helping me test Career Compass, a proposed LinkedIn experience for career development. This session will take about 30 minutes. We are testing the design. There are no right or wrong answers, and honest criticism is useful. You can skip any question or stop at any time.

“I would like to record audio so I can review your comments and share evidence of this session with my course reviewer. We will use the sharing method we just discussed. Please avoid mentioning confidential employer information or other people's personal details. Are you comfortable participating and being recorded?”

**Participant:** Wait for the actual answer. If permission is declined, do not record. Arrange a consenting participant for the required recorded session.

**Moderator, after permission and starting the recorder:**

“For the recording, this is the Career Compass design-sprint interview with participant P01. Today is [say the actual date]. Can you confirm that you agree to this audio recording and the sharing arrangement we discussed?”

**Participant:** Record the actual answer.

“Thank you. Some prototype features may not work. Please say what you are thinking as you use it. I will mostly observe and may ask follow-up questions. We can pause whenever you need.”

### 2. Background — 5 minutes

**Moderator:**

“Tell me a little about your current work and the kind of role you would like to move toward.”

**Participant:** Allow an uninterrupted response.

“Tell me about the last time you decided to develop a skill for your career.”

**Participant:** Allow an uninterrupted response.

“How did you decide which skill to work on?”

**Participant:** Allow an uninterrupted response.

“How did you choose what to do next? What alternatives did you consider?”

**Participant:** Allow an uninterrupted response.

“What happened after you made that choice?”

**Participant:** Allow an uninterrupted response.

Optional follow-up, if not already covered: “How, if at all, did LinkedIn or an online course fit into that process?”

**Moderator notes:** Record the participant's relevant profile and recent experience. Do not assume they share the fictional participant's needs.

### 3. First impression — 3 minutes

**Moderator:**

“I will now show you Career Compass. Before clicking anything, tell me what you think this experience is for.”

**Participant:** Describe the first impression in their own words.

“What would you expect to be able to do here?”

**Participant:** Describe expectations.

“Where would you begin?”

**Participant:** Explain or demonstrate the starting point.

**Moderator notes:** Capture what they notice, any uncertainty, and their first action. Do not explain the intended flow yet.

### 4. Task 1: Decide which skill to develop — 6 minutes

**Moderator:**

“Imagine you are considering a move into a Product Manager role and want to decide what to develop first. Use Career Compass to decide which skill you would work on. Please think aloud as you go.”

**Participant:** Use the prototype and describe their thinking. Do not read them a suggested answer.

After the attempt, ask:

“What led you to choose that skill?”

“What do you think the information on this screen is based on?”

“What, if anything, would you want to know before acting on it?”

“Was anything different from what you expected?”

**Moderator notes:** Record the chosen skill, path taken, any incorrect interpretation, and whether the task was completed independently. Note exact comments about recommendation evidence if the participant raises them.

### 5. Task 2: Choose an action and save a plan — 6 minutes

**Moderator:**

“You have the next 30 days to work on that skill alongside your normal commitments. Use the options available to choose an action you would realistically take and save a plan.”

**Participant:** Attempt the task and think aloud.

After the attempt, ask:

“How did you decide between the available options?”

“What information mattered to your decision?”

“What, if anything, was missing?”

“What do you think happened when you saved the plan?”

“What would you do next?”

**Moderator notes:** Record the chosen action, any comparison difficulties, whether saving succeeded, and what they believe the next step is. Distinguish participant comments from your interpretation.

### 6. Task 3: Return and record progress — 3 minutes

**Moderator:**

“Imagine a week has passed and you have completed the first activity. Show me how you would record your progress and find what to do next. Then show me how you would return to an earlier step if you wanted to reconsider your choice.”

**Participant:** Attempt the task and think aloud.

“What would you expect to happen when you return another day?”

**Moderator notes:** Record task completion and navigation problems. If the prototype cannot support an action, note the limitation and ask what the participant expected; do not count an unsupported interaction as a participant failure.

### 7. Wrap-up — 4 minutes

**Moderator:**

“Thinking about the whole experience, what worked well for you?”

**Participant:** Give their actual assessment.

“What did not work as you expected, or made the experience difficult?”

**Participant:** Give their actual assessment.

“What would you change or add? Why would that help?”

**Participant:** Suggest improvements, if any.

“How, if at all, would this fit into the way you plan your career today?”

**Participant:** Explain potential use or reasons not to use it.

“If you could change only one thing, which would you choose and why?”

**Participant:** State a priority, if they have one.

“Is there anything else you would like me to know?”

**Participant:** Add final comments.

“Thank you for your time and honest feedback. I will use what I observed to improve the design. That is the end of the session.”

Stop the recording.

### Neutral prompts during any task

Use these only when needed:

- “What are you thinking now?”
- “What would you expect that to do?”
- “Tell me more about that.”
- “What would you try next?”

If the participant gets stuck, allow time before helping. Record any help given and classify the attempt as assisted. Avoid suggesting expected findings, for example by asking whether the evidence is unclear or whether the participant wants a confirmation message.

## Audio storage and reviewer access

Recommended arrangement: keep the original recording in a private local backup and upload a review copy to a dedicated Google Drive folder named `Career Compass / Submission 2 / User research`. Use a neutral filename such as `Career_Compass_P01_YYYY-MM-DD.mp3`, replacing the date with the actual session date. An M4A recording is also fine if the reviewer can play it. Avoid putting the participant's full name in the filename.

For a reviewer who has not provided an email address, a direct file link with **Anyone with the link → Viewer** is straightforward. Anyone who receives that link can access the file, so agree to that sharing arrangement with the participant beforehand. If the participant requires restricted access, use **Restricted** and grant access to the reviewer's specified email instead.

Google Drive steps:

1. Upload the actual recording to the dedicated folder.
2. Right-click the audio file and select **Share**.
3. Under **General access**, choose **Anyone with the link** and set the role to **Viewer**, if that matches the agreed sharing arrangement.
4. Select **Copy link**, then **Done**.
5. Open the copied file link in a private/incognito browser window while signed out. Confirm that the correct audio opens and can be played or downloaded without requesting access.
6. Put the direct audio-file link in the session table above and on the presentation's interview-evidence slide. A clear label is **P01 interview audio — [actual session date], [actual duration]**. Add the same link to the final PDF after exporting and check that it still works.
7. Keep the recording available throughout the review and any resubmission period. Do not delete the file or revoke the agreed access while it is being reviewed.

Share the audio file itself rather than a folder containing unrelated material. A local Windows path will not give the remote reviewer access. If a work or school account prevents external link sharing, use an account that supports the agreed access or arrange access through the reviewer's email.

Official guidance: [Google Drive — Share and manage access to files and folders](https://support.google.com/drive/answer/7166529?co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop&hl=en). Google documents that Anyone with the link access does not require signing in to a Google Account and that a Viewer role can be selected.
