## Project Overview

The project for this course involves running a design sprint to bring your product from an idea into a fully thought-out concept with an interactive prototype that you can use to test and validate your concept with real users.

Generally, a design sprint is a cross-functional exercise with team members participating in a number of roles across a company, including design, product, engineering, marketing, and support. However, to allow flexibility for you to complete this project on your own timeline, you’ll go through this design sprint on your own.

To get started, you’ll set the stage by identifying why this is an important problem to solve.

## Design Sprint Phases

### Phase One: Understand

You’ll create a shared understanding of the problem space and identify a focus for the sprint. You’ll do this by completing a “How Might We” exercise and then using affinity mapping to group related ideas and create themes.

We’ll simulate completing the “How Might We” exercise as a group by combining your sticky notes with other “How Might We” sticky notes that we’ll pretend were written by teammates.

### Phase Two: Define

You’ll focus on the desired outcomes, which will allow you to work backwards and determine what needs to happen to achieve them. You’ll define goals, signals, and metrics, and draft a review of your product from a user’s perspective.

### Phase Three: Sketch

You’ll brainstorm many ideas and then select two to explore in more depth through comprehensive sketches.

### Phase Four: Decide

You’ll select the best idea to take through prototyping and validation.

### Phase Five: Prototype

You’ll turn your idea into something that feels real. You’ll start by creating a storyboard to map out the entire concept and all its interactions. Then, you’ll create a high-fidelity prototype based on your storyboard.

### Phase Six: Validate

You’ll create a research plan and conduct user interviews. You’ll learn whether your concept solves a user need and identify usability issues with your prototype.

It’s equally important to make sure that your concept can actually be built. You’ll prepare for a feasibility conversation with engineering.

## After the Design Sprint

Once all phases of the design sprint are complete, you’ll continue iterating on your idea. You’ll update your prototype based on user feedback and conduct one more user interview.

Lastly, you’ll prepare to hand off the product to the engineering team by writing detailed product requirements for your features.
